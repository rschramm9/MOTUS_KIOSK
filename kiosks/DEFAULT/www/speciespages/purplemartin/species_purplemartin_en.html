Purple Martin (Progne subis)

 

Purple Martins – Susan Young1

Description:   This is the largest swallow in North America, measuring 7 to 9 inches long and weighing 1.5 to 2 ounces. It has a slightly-hooked short beak, broad chest, forked tail, and long tapered wings. Adult male feathers are dark blackish-blue with an iridescent sheen creating a deep purple appearance. Female and juvenile plumage is a duller blue and gray.

 

Migration & Habitat:  Purple martins prefer large, open areas near water with adequate perching structures for foraging. They also establish colonies at sites where nest boxes are provided, such as in the Willamette Valley. In the breeding season they live along the Pacific coast and throughout eastern North America. They migrate long distances spending the winter in South America.

Range Map: BirdLife International (2025) Legend: Green: Resident Yellow: Breeding Blue: Passage 2

 

Reproduction:   They prefer nesting in groups and build their nests inside sheltered cavities found in trees, cliffs, cacti, gourds, or birdhouses. Their slightly flat nest is composed of twigs, plant stems, grass, and mud, then lined with fresh green leaves. They produce one brood of 3 to 6 eggs per year and return to the same nesting site if the breeding season was successful.

 

Diet:  Purple Martins are aerial insectivores that consume a wide-variety of insects caught midair. They prefer larger flying insects, like dragonflies, flies, and moths. Their diet provides a natural pest control in agricultural and urban areas.

 

Conservation Status:  Their global status is Least Concern, but Oregon and Washington consider them a regional concern due to limited breeding habitat.

 

Fun Facts:

  • Their time spent on the ground is limited to collecting nesting material or picking up grit that helps digest food.
  • Purple Martins drink in flight by skimming the surface of water with their lower bills.
  • Invasive fire ants are one of their favorite foods and they eat billions of queens each year.
  • If weather is cold and rainy, they risk of starvation when their primary food source, flying insects, stop coming out.
